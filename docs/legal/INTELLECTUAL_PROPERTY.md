# CorpusMiner - Intellectual Property and Content Rules

Status: Product policy/engineering guidance; binding language requires legal review.

## 1. Product ownership

CorpusMiner is presented as a product of Askit4.

The exact legal entity that owns the software, trademarks, website content, and contractual rights MUST be confirmed before publishing binding legal notices. The current public Askit4 website uses both `Askit4` and `ASKITFOR` naming.

## 2. Repository and software

Unless an explicit open-source license states otherwise:
- CorpusMiner source code is proprietary;
- product-specific designs, logos, icons, copy, workflows, and documentation are protected assets;
- no agent should add an open-source license to the repository without explicit owner approval;
- no agent should change copyright ownership statements without explicit owner approval.

## 3. User-uploaded content

Users may import bibliographic exports, documents, metadata, or other research material only when they have the rights/permissions necessary for the intended use.

The product should not claim ownership of user-uploaded source content merely because it is processed by CorpusMiner.

Terms should grant the operator only the limited rights necessary to host, copy, transform, index, analyze, and generate outputs from user content for providing the service, subject to the final legal model.

## 4. Derived data and analyses

The legal treatment of normalized metadata, deduplicated records, embeddings, summaries, classifications, networks, and other derived outputs should be explicitly addressed in Terms before commercialization.

Do not assume all derived data is free of third-party database, copyright, license, confidentiality, or contractual restrictions.

## 5. Scopus and Web of Science

Scopus and Web of Science are third-party services/brands. CorpusMiner must not imply affiliation, sponsorship, certification, or endorsement unless documented contractually.

Users are responsible for complying with the license/terms applicable to their access and exports from those services.

Product UI should use descriptive source names only as necessary for interoperability and provenance.

## 6. Academic papers and abstracts

Bibliographic metadata and article content are not legally equivalent.

Do not assume that access to a citation, abstract, or PDF grants unrestricted rights to reproduce, redistribute, train models on, or publish that content.

When CorpusMiner stores or processes full text, the product must preserve access controls and should provide configuration appropriate to licensed/private material.

## 7. Logos and trademarks

Official product/corporate assets:
- CorpusMiner assets: `assets/brand/logos/`
- Askit4 endorsement logo: `assets/brand/logos/askit4-logo.png`

Do not redraw, alter, or generate substitute logos for production use.

Third-party names/logos must not be used beyond what is permitted for identification/interoperability without appropriate permission.

## 8. Generated content and AI

If AI generates summaries, categorizations, code, or analytical narratives:
- preserve source provenance where relevant;
- avoid representing AI-generated conclusions as source facts;
- document external provider use;
- do not assume AI output is automatically free of third-party rights.

## 9. Open-source dependencies

Maintain dependency/license awareness. Before distribution or commercial deployment:
- identify licenses that require attribution;
- identify copyleft/restrictive obligations;
- retain required notices;
- avoid copying third-party source snippets into proprietary code without compatible licensing.
